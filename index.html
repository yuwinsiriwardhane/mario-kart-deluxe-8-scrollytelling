Moving to the start of the 21st century the "Need For Speed" franchise moved into homes allowing gamers to race from the comfort of there couch. However during this time a new racing game was being developed in Japan. This game series would change not only the racing game sphere but entirity of video games itself with its creative mechanics and likeability by all ages. This game is Mario Kart.

Game Play Mechanics

Mario Kart Item Distributions

How's it all work?

Items in Mario Kart 8 Deluxe work in one of the most complex ways out of all Mario Kart games. In past installments, such as Mario Kart 8 on the Wii U, the game had items chosen based solely on distance. This is not how it works in Mario Kart 8 Deluxe (MK8DX). In fact, items are chosen based on distance AND placement. Most casual players probably had the conception that items were based on place. I'm here to say that these players weren't (entirely) wrong. Items are chosen based on placement categories as seen in the diagram above. Being in the last 4 placements (9th-12th) yield a much stronger set of items. That being said, those in 3rd-5th can still get some of these items if the player in 1st is extremely far ahead, just try changing the Placement dropdown categories and see how much better the items are!

An additional feature that determines item distribution is also time. More powerful items appear in the item pool after 30 seconds have elapsed in a race. Displayed above are the item distributions after 30 seconds have passed since this is mostly what matters. It is important to know that the Lightning, Bullet Bill, Spiny Shell (Blue Shell), Golden Mushroom, and Crazy Eight do NOT appear in the item pool until 30 seconds have passed.

What gives?

Knowing these distributions plays a key part in competitive Mario Kart play. Now while the game is inheritly driven by luck, knowing these probabilities helps create advantages where there may seem to be disadvantages. Some frequent Mario Kart players may be familiar with the term "bagging". This is a playstyle that takes advantage of the 9th-12th place item ranges in particular. By knowing that you get better items there, players may purposely fall behind towards to beginning of the race and then make a major comeback with the powerful items obtained from being behind. Items like the Super Star or Bullet Bill can cover large distances quickly, and can get through off-road regions.

Now this doesn't mean being in 1st place is bad. Frontrunning is what many competitive players have called the chase for 1st place. Securing 1st can allow the player to escape the chaos of players fighting behind them. With that being said, 1st place still requires knowledge of item distributions. One additional feature of item distributions is that having an item in your hand means your second item cannot be the same item. Since you can hold two items at once, this encourages effective cycling of item boxes. As you may see in the pie chart, being in 1st place makes the Coin powerup very common. This may seem bad since its not that useful compared to other items, but by holding onto the Coin powerup you can cycle for another unique item. The Super Horn is one of the best items with a low (but not zero) chance to be obtained. Getting this in 1st place is the player's only realistic way to evade getting hit by the Spiny Shell (which always targets 1st place). In general, being in 1st means the player will get many red shells sent at them by 2nd place, so a defensive item like a Banana or Green Shell is necessary.

Mario Kart Hidden Stats

Select a character

Why do these matter?

These hidden statistics vary in importance, but all have applications throughout various tracks in the game. The statistics you see in the diagram above are not shown in game whatsoever, which is a questionable decision considering how importance some of them are. Each character in Mario Kart has their own set of statistics in addition to the stats gained from choosing your kart, wheels, and glider. In the actual game when selecting your character and combination you see five main stats. These are are on-road traction, weight, speed, acceleration and handling. The hidden stats of the game are: mini-turbo, off-road traction, invincibility, air speed, water speed, anti-gravity speed, air acceleration, water acceleration, and anti-gravity acceleration. As you can see, there are a HANDFUL of hidden stats which may seem overwhemling at first but will be explained shortly!

The Important Stats

There are two very important stats that are hidden. These are what's known as Mini-Turbo and Invincibility. Mini-turbo is by far the most important hidden stat, and arguably the most important stat in the ENTIRE GAME despite it being hidden from us. So why's it so important. Well if you've ever drifted in Mario Kart or done those special 'tricks' off of slopes or edges, then this stat has played a role. The mini-turbo stat actually affects several things:

  • How long it takes to charge a drift
  • How fast a drift charge goes when you release it
  • How long the drift speed boost lasts
  • How fast you go after doing a trick
  • How long the speed boost of doing a trick lasts

    • As one could notice, these are a lot of very crucial mechanics which aren't directly communicated with the player when choosing your character. Knowing this information might make you the most hated in your friend group as you suddenly become better at the game.

    Now the other important stat is Invincibility. It is no where near as important as mini-turbo but still is the next most useful hidden stat. Invincibility is also leagues simpler than mini-turbo. If you've ever noticed that you got hit by several shells or other terrible things in a row but only got 'stunned' once, that would be invincibility playing its part. Every deadly weapon in Mario Kart that can knock you down has an individual amount of time in which it renders you immune to taking a hit again. The invincibility stat simply determines how much longer this immune time lasts. This stat actually didn't exist in early versions of Mario Kart 8 Deluxe, but was added to balance out some characters. So choosing a character like 'Petey Piranha' gives much more invincibility while a character like 'Yoshi' gets some of the lowest. Highly competitive players, or just tryhards in your friendgroup, might use this stat to purposely get hit by weaker items like a Banana to go through higher-tier threats such as the Spiny Shell. Generally speaking, the invincibility stat can be somewhat helpful when the player is stuck in the middle of the pack with several players sending items at one another as it may be the difference in getting hit once instead of twice.

    Niche Stats

    The other stats seen are very specific to tracks. The Off-Road Traction concerns how much you slip on special road types like sand or ice. In general, having a higher value of this stat makes it easier to control your kart on special road types. Then there is Air Acceleration which affects how fast you speed up and slow down specifically in gliders. There is also Air Speed which is just simply your max speed while in a glider. Now These specific stats actually have even more variants which I didn't like since they do NOT change based on character but instead only change based on the kart, wheels and glider that the players chooses. These extra stats are Water Speed, Water Acceleration, Anti-Gravity Speed, and Anti-Gravity Acceleration which all just affect your speed based on those terrain types in the game. As it turns out, even the regular Ground Speed and Ground Acceleration stat which is used in the majority of tracks is the same as all those other speeds and accelerations for each characters. It is solely up to the kart, wheels and gliders chosen to vary these terrain-specific stats. In the general case, these stats are ignored.
